Tabla

The tabla is a pair of drums that forms the backbone of Dayro music. It consists of two drums: the smaller dayan and the larger bayan. The tabla player, known as a "tablavadak," creates intricate rhythms that drive the Dayro performance

The dayan is made of wood and produces higher-pitched sounds, while the bayan is made of metal and produces deeper tones. The combination of these two drums creates a rich, complex rhythmic foundation for Dayro music.

Tabla players in Dayro culture are highly respected for their ability to maintain complex rhythms while supporting the vocal performance. They often engage in "jugalbandi" with the main singer, creating an exciting music.

Benjo

Benjo originated in Africa and was popularized in America, it has been adapted and integrated into Gujarati folk music, creating a distinctive fusion sound.

Players often use a combination of strumming and fingerpicking techniques to create complex, syncopated rhythms that drive the performance forward. The instrument's distinctive sound helps create a lively, energetic atmosphere that is characteristic of many Dayro gatherings.
